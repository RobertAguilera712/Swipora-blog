Title: Skill 24: Parallel Structure with Coordinate Conjunctions
Date: 2026-09-16
Category: Advanced Grammar
Tags: Advanced Grammar, TOEFL Preparation, Parallel Structure
Summary: Learn to use and, but, and or with balanced grammar.

Coordinate conjunctions join words, phrases, or clauses. The most common ones in TOEFL grammar questions are **and**, **but**, and **or**. When a conjunction joins two parts of a sentence, those parts should have the same grammatical form. This is called **parallel structure**.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
:x: I like <span class='grammar-blue'>to read</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>and</span> <span class='grammar-red'>writing</span>.<br>
:white_check_mark: I like <span class='grammar-blue'>to read</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>and</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>to write</span>.<br>
:white_check_mark: I like <span class='grammar-red'>reading</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>and</span> <span class='grammar-red'>writing</span>.
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-blue'>Infinitive: to + base verb.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-red'>Gerund: verb + ing.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Coordinate conjunction.</span>**
</sub>

The verb **like** can be followed by either an infinitive or a gerund. However, when **and** connects two activities, both activities need the same form. Do not join an infinitive with a gerund.

## Adjectives joined with coordinate conjunctions

<div class="grammar">
She is <span class='grammar-pink'>smart</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>and</span> <span class='grammar-pink'>beautiful</span>.<br>
The car is not <span class='grammar-pink'>fast</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>but</span> <span class='grammar-pink'>efficient</span>.<br>
She may be <span class='grammar-pink'>angry</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>or</span> <span class='grammar-pink'>sad</span>.
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-pink'>Adjective.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Coordinate conjunction.</span>**
</sub>

Each conjunction joins two adjectives. The sentence remains balanced because both sides describe the same noun.

## Nouns joined with coordinate conjunctions

<div class="grammar">
I am an <span class='grammar-orange'>engineer</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>and</span> a <span class='grammar-orange'>teacher</span>.<br>
She is not a <span class='grammar-orange'>nurse</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>but</span> a <span class='grammar-orange'>doctor</span>.<br>
We need to talk to the <span class='grammar-orange'>coordinator</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>or</span> to the <span class='grammar-orange'>assistant</span>.
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-orange'>Noun or noun phrase.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Coordinate conjunction.</span>**
</sub>

## Verbs joined with coordinate conjunctions

<div class="grammar">
I <span class='grammar-red'>cooked</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>and</span> <span class='grammar-red'>ate</span> pizza yesterday.<br>
He does not <span class='grammar-red'>clean</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>but</span> <span class='grammar-red'>cooks</span> for her.<br>
You can <span class='grammar-red'>run</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>or</span> <span class='grammar-red'>walk</span> during the race.
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-red'>Verb.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Coordinate conjunction.</span>**
</sub>

## Two clauses joined with coordinate conjunctions

A **clause** has its own subject and verb. When **and**, **but**, or **or** joins two independent clauses, use a comma before the conjunction.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
:white_check_mark: <span class='grammar-green'>The researcher</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>collected</span> the data, <span class='grammar-purple'>and</span> <span class='grammar-green'>she</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>analyzed</span> it carefully.<br>
:white_check_mark: <span class='grammar-green'>The lecture</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>was</span> long, <span class='grammar-purple'>but</span> <span class='grammar-green'>the students</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>remained</span> interested.<br>
:white_check_mark: <span class='grammar-green'>You</span> can <span class='grammar-blue'>submit</span> the form today, <span class='grammar-purple'>or</span> <span class='grammar-green'>you</span> can <span class='grammar-blue'>submit</span> it tomorrow.
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-green'>Subject.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-blue'>Verb.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Coordinate conjunction.</span>**
</sub>

<br>
Each side of the conjunction can stand alone as a complete sentence:

<div class="grammar">
<span class='grammar-green'>The researcher</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>collected</span> the data.<br>
<span class='grammar-green'>She</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>analyzed</span> it carefully.
</div>
<br>

Do not use a comma if the two verbs share the same subject.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
:x: <span class='grammar-green'>The researcher</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>collected</span> the data, <span class='grammar-purple'>and</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>analyzed</span> it carefully.<br>
:white_check_mark: <span class='grammar-green'>The researcher</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>collected</span> the data <span class='grammar-purple'>and</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>analyzed</span> it carefully.
</div>

In the correct sentence, **the researcher** is the subject of both verbs. The second part is not an independent clause, so no comma is needed.

## Phrases joined with coordinate conjunctions

Conjunctions can join longer units too. The two phrases should still match.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
:x: The students were asked <span class='grammar-blue'>to complete the survey</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>and</span> <span class='grammar-red'>submitting it online</span>.<br>
:white_check_mark: The students were asked <span class='grammar-blue'>to complete the survey</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>and</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>to submit it online</span>.
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-blue'>Infinitive phrase.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-red'>Gerund phrase.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Coordinate conjunction.</span>**
</sub>

## TOEFL strategy

When you see **and**, **but**, or **or**, look at the grammar immediately before and after the conjunction. The two parts should match: adjective + adjective, noun + noun, verb + verb, clause + clause, or phrase + phrase.
