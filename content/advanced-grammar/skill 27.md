Title: Skill 27: Form Comparatives and Superlatives Correctly
Date: 2026-09-17
Category: Advanced Grammar
Tags: Advanced Grammar, TOEFL Preparation, Comparatives, Superlatives
Summary: Learn to form and use comparative and superlative adjectives correctly.

Use a **comparative adjective** to compare two nouns. Use a **superlative adjective** to describe one noun as having the highest or lowest degree of a quality in a group.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
Comparative: <span class='grammar-orange'>Mia</span> is <span class='grammar-green'>short</span><span class='grammar-red'>er</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>than</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>Leo</span>.<br>
Superlative: <span class='grammar-orange'>Mia</span> is <span class='grammar-red'>the</span> <span class='grammar-green'>short</span><span class='grammar-red'>est</span> student <span class='grammar-pink'>in the class</span>.
</div>

## One syllable adjectives: add *-er* or *-est*

For many short adjectives, add **-er** to compare two nouns and **-est** to describe one noun in a group. Put **the** before the superlative adjective.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
<span class='grammar-green'>fast</span> → <span class='grammar-green'>fast</span><span class='grammar-red'>er</span> → <span class='grammar-red'>the</span> <span class='grammar-green'>fast</span><span class='grammar-red'>est</span><br>
<span class='grammar-green'>small</span> → <span class='grammar-green'>small</span><span class='grammar-red'>er</span> → <span class='grammar-red'>the</span> <span class='grammar-green'>small</span><span class='grammar-red'>est</span><br>
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-green'>Base adjective.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-red'>Comparative or superlative.</span>**<br>
</sub>

For example, a car can be **faster than** a bike, while one car can be **the fastest** car in a race.

## Two syllable adjectives ending in *-y*: change *y* to *-ier* or *-iest*

When an adjective ends in a consonant plus **-y**, change **y** to **i** before adding **-er** or **-est**. Use **the** before the superlative form.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
<span class='grammar-green'>easy</span> → <span class='grammar-green'>eas</span><span class='grammar-red'>ier</span> → <span class='grammar-red'>the</span> <span class='grammar-green'>eas</span><span class='grammar-red'>iest</span><br>
<span class='grammar-green'>happy</span> → <span class='grammar-green'>happ</span><span class='grammar-red'>ier</span> → <span class='grammar-red'>the</span> <span class='grammar-green'>happ</span><span class='grammar-red'>iest</span><br>
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-green'>Base adjective.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-red'>Comparative or superlative.</span>**<br>
</sub>

For example, one child can be **happier than** another child, while one can be **the happiest** child in a class.

## Longer adjectives: use *more* and *most*

With many adjectives of two or more syllables, put **more** or **the most** before the adjective. For example, use **more beautiful** and **the most beautiful**.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
<span class='grammar-orange'>The rose</span> is <span class='grammar-red'>more</span> <span class='grammar-green'>beautiful</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>than</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>the daisy</span>.<br>
<span class='grammar-orange'>The rose</span> is <span class='grammar-red'>the most</span> <span class='grammar-green'>beautiful</span> flower <span class='grammar-pink'>in the garden</span>.<br>
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-orange'>Noun phrases.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-red'>Comparative or superlative word.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-green'>Adjective.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Comparison word.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-pink'>Comparison group.</span>**
</sub>

**Beautiful** keeps its spelling. **More** makes the comparative adjective, and **the most** makes the superlative adjective. Do not mix **-er** or **-est** with **More** and **The most**.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
:x: <span class='grammar-orange'>The bedroom</span> is <span class='grammar-red'>more</span> <span class='grammar-green'>small</span><span class='grammar-red'>er</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>than</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>the living room</span>.<br>
:white_check_mark: <span class='grammar-orange'>The bedroom</span> is <span class='grammar-green'>small</span><span class='grammar-red'>er</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>than</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>the living room</span>.<br>

:x: <span class='grammar-orange'>The red car</span> is <span class='grammar-red'>the most</span> <span class='grammar-green'>fast</span><span class='grammar-red'>est</span> car <span class='grammar-pink'>in the race</span>.<br>
:white_check_mark: <span class='grammar-orange'>The red car</span> is <span class='grammar-red'>the</span> <span class='grammar-green'>fast</span><span class='grammar-red'>est</span> car <span class='grammar-pink'>in the race</span>.<br>
</div>

## Irregular forms

Some common words do not follow the usual spelling rules. Learn their comparative and superlative forms as separate words.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
<span class='grammar-green'>good</span> → <span class='grammar-red'>better</span> → <span class='grammar-red'>the best</span><br>
<span class='grammar-green'>bad</span> → <span class='grammar-red'>worse</span> → <span class='grammar-red'>the worst</span><br>
<span class='grammar-green'>much / many</span> → <span class='grammar-red'>more</span> → <span class='grammar-red'>the most</span><br>
<span class='grammar-green'>little</span> → <span class='grammar-red'>less</span> → <span class='grammar-red'>the least</span>
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-green'>Base form.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-red'>Comparative or superlative form.</span>**
</sub>

## TOEFL strategy

Look for **than** with a comparative adjective and usually **the** with a superlative adjective. Use only one form: **smaller**, not *more smaller*; **the fastest**, not *the most fastest*. Check that the nouns in a comparison name the same kind of thing.
