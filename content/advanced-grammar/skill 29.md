Title: Skill 29: Use the “The More …, the More …” Structure
Slug: skill-29-use-the-the-more-the-more-structure
Date: 2026-09-19
Category: Advanced Grammar
Tags: Advanced Grammar, TOEFL Preparation, Parallel Comparatives
Summary: Learn how paired comparatives express a relationship between two changing situations.

Paired comparatives show that **as one thing changes, another changes too**. The pattern is **the + comparative + clause, the + comparative + clause**. A **clause** has a subject and a verb. The two clauses can show a cause and result or simply two changes that happen together.

## Build the two clauses

Each half needs its own **the** and a comparative expression. A comma usually separates the two clauses.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
<span class='grammar-purple'>The</span> <span class='grammar-red'>harder</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>you study</span>, <span class='grammar-purple'>the</span> <span class='grammar-red'>better</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>your grades get</span>.<br>
<span class='grammar-purple'>The</span> <span class='grammar-red'>bigger</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>a box is</span>, <span class='grammar-purple'>the</span> <span class='grammar-red'>more</span> space <span class='grammar-orange'>it needs</span>.
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>The: word that begins each half of the pattern.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-red'>Comparative adjective, adverb, or determiner.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-orange'>Subject and verb of the clause.</span>**
</sub>

In the first sentence, **harder** is a comparative adverb that describes **study**, and **better** is a comparative adjective that describes **grades**. In the second, **bigger** is a comparative adjective and **more** is a comparative determiner before the noun **space**. Each half has its own subject and verb: **you study / your grades get** and **a box is / it needs**.

## Use comparatives with nouns

The comparative can also come before a noun: **more books**, **fewer games**, or **less time**. These words are **comparative determiners** because they tell us how much or how many. Use **fewer** with countable plural nouns and **less** with uncountable nouns.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
<span class='grammar-purple'>The</span> <span class='grammar-red'>more</span> <span class='grammar-green'>books</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>you have</span>, <span class='grammar-purple'>the</span> <span class='grammar-red'>more</span> <span class='grammar-green'>space</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>you need</span>.<br>
<span class='grammar-purple'>The</span> <span class='grammar-red'>less</span> <span class='grammar-green'>time</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>you have</span>, <span class='grammar-purple'>the</span> <span class='grammar-red'>fewer</span> <span class='grammar-green'>games</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>you can play</span>.
</div>

<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>The: word that begins each half of the pattern.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-red'>Comparative determiner.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-green'>Noun.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-orange'>Subject and verb of the clause.</span>**
</sub>

**Books** and **games** are countable plural nouns. **Space** and **time** are uncountable nouns. That is why the second example uses **less time** but **fewer games**.

## Avoid common structure errors

Do not replace the second **the** with **than** or leave it out. In this pattern, **the** introduces both changing situations.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
:x: <span class='grammar-purple'>The</span> <span class='grammar-red'>more</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>you read</span>, <span class='grammar-purple'>than</span> <span class='grammar-red'>more</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>you learn</span>.<br>
:white_check_mark: <span class='grammar-purple'>The</span> <span class='grammar-red'>more</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>you read</span>, <span class='grammar-purple'>the</span> <span class='grammar-red'>more</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>you learn</span>.<br>
:x: <span class='grammar-purple'>The</span> <span class='grammar-red'>less</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>you sleep</span>, <span class='grammar-red'>more tired</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>you feel</span>.<br>
:white_check_mark: <span class='grammar-purple'>The</span> <span class='grammar-red'>less</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>you sleep</span>, <span class='grammar-purple'>the</span> <span class='grammar-red'>more tired</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>you feel</span>.
</div>

## TOEFL strategy

When a sentence begins **the + comparative**, look for a second **the + comparative** after the comma. Check that each clause has a subject and verb. If the comparative comes before a noun, check whether the noun is countable or uncountable.
