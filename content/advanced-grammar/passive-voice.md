Title: Passive Voice
Date: 2026-10-05
Category: Advanced Grammar
Tags: Advanced Grammar, Passive Voice, Active Voice, Verb To Be, Past Participles
Summary: Learn how to form and use the passive voice in the most common English tenses.

The **passive voice** focuses on the person or thing that receives an action. We often use it when the person performing the action is unknown, unimportant, obvious, or less important than the result. The **active voice** focuses on who or what performs the action.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
Active voice:<br>
<span class='grammar-green'>The chef</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>prepares</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>the soup</span>.<br><br>
Passive voice:<br>
<span class='grammar-green'>The soup</span> <span class='grammar-pink'>is</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>prepared</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>by the chef</span>.
</div>

<sub>**\*<span class='grammar-green'>Subject.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-blue'>Main verb or past participle.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-orange'>Object.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-pink'>Verb to be or auxiliary verb.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Agent.</span>**</sub>

Both sentences describe the same action, but they organize the information differently. In the active sentence, **the chef** performs the action. In the passive sentence, **the soup** receives the action and becomes the subject.

## When do we use the passive voice?

Use the passive voice when the agent is unknown, obvious, or less important than the action or result.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
<span class='grammar-green'>My phone</span> <span class='grammar-pink'>was</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>stolen</span>.<br>
<span class='grammar-green'>The vegetables</span> <span class='grammar-pink'>are</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>washed</span> before cooking.<br>
<span class='grammar-green'>The patient</span> <span class='grammar-pink'>was</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>treated</span> at the hospital.
</div>

<sub>**\*<span class='grammar-green'>Subject that receives the action.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-pink'>Verb to be.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-blue'>Past participle.</span>**</sub>

## Active and passive structures

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
Active: <span class='grammar-green'>subject</span> + <span class='grammar-blue'>verb</span> + <span class='grammar-orange'>object</span><br>
Passive: <span class='grammar-green'>new subject</span> + <span class='grammar-pink'>be</span> + <span class='grammar-blue'>past participle</span> + <span class='grammar-purple'>by + agent</span>
</div>

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
Active: <span class='grammar-green'>The baker</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>bakes</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>the bread</span>.<br>
Passive: <span class='grammar-green'>The bread</span> <span class='grammar-pink'>is</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>baked</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>by the baker</span>.
</div>

<sub>**\*<span class='grammar-green'>Subject.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-blue'>Main verb or past participle.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-orange'>Object.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-pink'>Verb to be.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Optional agent.</span>**</sub>

The **by + agent** phrase is optional. Include it only when the identity of the agent is useful or important.

## Past participles

Every passive verb contains a past participle. Regular past participles normally end in **-ed**. Irregular forms must be learned.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
<span class='grammar-orange'>prepare</span> → <span class='grammar-blue'>prepared</span><br>
<span class='grammar-orange'>wash</span> → <span class='grammar-blue'>washed</span><br>
<span class='grammar-orange'>make</span> → <span class='grammar-blue'>made</span><br>
<span class='grammar-orange'>write</span> → <span class='grammar-blue'>written</span><br>
<span class='grammar-orange'>eat</span> → <span class='grammar-blue'>eaten</span>
</div>

<sub>**\*<span class='grammar-orange'>Base verb.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-blue'>Past participle.</span>**</sub>

## Passive voice in common tenses

The past participle stays the same. The auxiliary verbs change to show the tense.

## Conclusion

In the active voice, the subject performs the action. In the passive voice, the subject receives it. To form the passive voice correctly, use the appropriate form of **be** followed by the past participle. Focus on the action or result, and include **by + agent** only when the person or thing performing the action is important.
