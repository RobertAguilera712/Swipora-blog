Title: Skill 25: Parallel Structure with Paired Conjunctions
Date: 2026-09-16
Category: Advanced Grammar
Tags: Advanced Grammar, TOEFL Preparation, Parallel Structure
Summary: Learn how to use paired conjunctions with balanced grammar.

Paired conjunctions come in two parts. They connect two equal grammatical ideas, so the words after each part must have the same form. In other words, the sentence must be **parallel** or balanced.

<div class="grammar">
<span class='grammar-purple'>both ... and</span><br>
<span class='grammar-purple'>either ... or</span><br>
<span class='grammar-purple'>neither ... nor</span><br>
<span class='grammar-purple'>not only ... but also</span>
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Paired conjunction.</span>**
</sub>

## Match adjective with adjective

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
:x: The new course is <span class='grammar-purple'>both</span> <span class='grammar-pink'>challenging</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>and</span> <span class='grammar-red'>makes me get bored</span>.<br>
:white_check_mark: The new course is <span class='grammar-purple'>both</span> <span class='grammar-pink'>challenging</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>and</span> <span class='grammar-pink'>boring</span>.
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-pink'>Adjective.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-red'>Verb phrase.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Paired conjunction.</span>**
</sub>

In the incorrect sentence, **challenging** is an adjective, but **makes me bored** is a verb phrase. The corrected sentence joins two adjectives.

## Match infinitive with infinitive

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
:x: The professor asked us <span class='grammar-purple'>not only</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>to read</span> the article <span class='grammar-purple'>but also</span> <span class='grammar-red'>writing</span> a review.<br>
:white_check_mark: The professor asked us <span class='grammar-purple'>not only</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>to read</span> the article <span class='grammar-purple'>but also</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>to write</span> a review.
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-blue'>Infinitive: to + base verb.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-red'>Gerund: verb + ing.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Paired conjunction.</span>**
</sub>

## Match noun with noun

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
The scholarship is available to <span class='grammar-purple'>either</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>undergraduate students</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>or</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>graduate students</span>.<br>
The experiment showed <span class='grammar-purple'>neither</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>a clear cause</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>nor</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>a simple solution</span>.
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-orange'>Noun phrase.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Paired conjunction.</span>**
</sub>

## TOEFL strategy

Find the first part of the pair—for example, **either** or **not only**—and then find its partner. Check the two elements it connects: they should be the same type of grammar, such as noun + noun, adjective + adjective, or infinitive + infinitive.
