Title: Adjective Clause 
Date: 2026-09-22
Category: Advanced Grammar
Tags: Advanced Grammar, Adjective Clauses
Summary: Learn to describe nouns with adjective clauses.

An **adjective clause** is a group of words that describes a noun. It usually comes immediately after the noun that it describes.

Adjective clauses help us combine two short sentences. This makes our English clearer and more natural.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
<span class='grammar-orange'>The chef</span> is my teacher.<br>
<span class='grammar-green'>He</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>makes</span> excellent bread.<br><br>
<span class='grammar-orange'>The chef</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>who</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>makes</span> excellent bread is my teacher.
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-orange'>Noun.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-green'>Subject.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-blue'>Verb.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Relative word.</span>**
</sub>

In the combined sentence, **who makes excellent bread** describes **the chef**. The word **who** replaces **he**.

## Use *who* for people

Use **who** after a noun that refers to a person. In this pattern, **who** is followed by a verb.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
<span class='grammar-orange'>Person</span> + <span class='grammar-purple'>who</span> + <span class='grammar-blue'>verb</span>
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-orange'>Noun.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Relative word.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-blue'>Verb.</span>**
</sub>

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
<span class='grammar-orange'>A baker</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>who</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>makes</span> bread works here.<br>
<span class='grammar-orange'>The server</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>who</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>helps</span> us is friendly.<br>
<span class='grammar-orange'>The student</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>who</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>cuts</span> the vegetables is careful.
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-orange'>Noun.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Relative word.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-blue'>Verb.</span>**
</sub>

The adjective clauses tell us which baker, server, or student we mean.

## Use *which* for things

Use **which** after a noun that refers to a thing. Foods, ingredients, tools, and machines are things.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
<span class='grammar-orange'>Thing</span> + <span class='grammar-purple'>which</span> + <span class='grammar-blue'>verb</span>
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-orange'>Noun.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Relative word.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-blue'>Verb.</span>**
</sub>

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
<span class='grammar-orange'>The soup</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>which</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>contains</span> cream is <span class='grammar-pink'>hot</span>.<br>
<span class='grammar-orange'>The knife</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>which</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>cuts</span> the bread is <span class='grammar-pink'>sharp</span>.<br>
<span class='grammar-orange'>The oven</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>which</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>heats</span> the pizza is <span class='grammar-pink'>new</span>.
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-orange'>Noun.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Relative word.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-blue'>Verb.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-pink'>Adjective.</span>**
</sub>

## Use *that* for people or things

In simple adjective clauses, **that** can replace **who** or **which**. This is common in everyday English.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
<span class='grammar-orange'>The cook</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>that</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>prepares</span> the sauce is my classmate.<br>
<span class='grammar-orange'>The sauce</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>that</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>contains</span> chili is <span class='grammar-pink'>spicy</span>.<br>
<span class='grammar-orange'>The cake</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>that</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>has</span> chocolate is <span class='grammar-pink'>delicious</span>.
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-orange'>Noun.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Relative word.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-blue'>Verb.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-pink'>Adjective.</span>**
</sub>

For now, remember this simple guide:

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
People → <span class='grammar-purple'>who</span> or <span class='grammar-purple'>that</span><br>
Things → <span class='grammar-purple'>which</span> or <span class='grammar-purple'>that</span>
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Relative word.</span>**
</sub>

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
<span class='grammar-orange'>Place</span> + <span class='grammar-purple'>where</span> + <span class='grammar-green'>subject</span> + <span class='grammar-blue'>verb</span>
</div>

**\*<span class='grammar-orange'>Noun.</span>**<br>

**\*<span class='grammar-blue'>Verb.</span>**

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
<span class='grammar-orange'>The kitchen</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>where</span> <span class='grammar-green'>we</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>practice</span> is <span class='grammar-pink'>clean</span>.<br>

## Use *when* for moments

## Put the adjective clause after the noun

The adjective clause needs to be close to the noun that it describes. This helps the reader understand the sentence.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">

:white_check_mark: <span class='grammar-orange'>The pizza</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>that</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>contains</span> mushrooms is <span class='grammar-pink'>good</span>.<br><br>
:x: <span class='grammar-orange'>The chef</span> is my teacher <span class='grammar-purple'>who</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>makes</span> bread.<br>
:white_check_mark: <span class='grammar-orange'>The chef</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>who</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>makes</span> bread is my teacher.
</div>

**\*<span class='grammar-pink'>Adjective.</span>**
</sub>

## Do not repeat the subject

When **who**, **which**, or **that** is the subject of the adjective clause, do not add another subject immediately after it.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
:x: <span class='grammar-orange'>The cook</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>who</span> <span class='grammar-green'>he</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>makes</span> pasta is Italian.<br>
:white_check_mark: <span class='grammar-orange'>The cook</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>who</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>makes</span> pasta is Italian.<br><br>
:x: <span class='grammar-orange'>The blender</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>that</span> <span class='grammar-green'>it</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>mixes</span> the sauce is <span class='grammar-pink'>new</span>.<br>
:white_check_mark: <span class='grammar-orange'>The blender</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>that</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>mixes</span> the sauce is <span class='grammar-pink'>new</span>.

## Choose the correct relative word

Ask one simple question: does the noun name a person, a thing, or a place?

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">

:white_check_mark: The baker <span class='grammar-purple'>who</span> makes the cake is my friend.<br><br>
:x: The pan <span class='grammar-purple'>who</span> is on the stove is hot.<br>
:white_check_mark: The pan <span class='grammar-purple'>which</span> is on the stove is hot.<br><br>
:x: The café <span class='grammar-purple'>who</span> we eat lunch is small.<br>
:white_check_mark: The café <span class='grammar-purple'>where</span> we eat lunch is small.

## Quick review

An adjective clause describes a noun. Place it directly after that noun. Use **who** for people, **which** for things, **that** for people or things, **where** for places, and **when** for times or moments.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
<span class='grammar-orange'>The chef</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>who</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>works</span> here is friendly.<br>
<span class='grammar-orange'>The dish</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>that</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>contains</span> fish is popular.<br>
<span class='grammar-orange'>The kitchen</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>where</span> <span class='grammar-green'>we</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>study</span> is upstairs.<br>
<span class='grammar-orange'>The time</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>when</span> <span class='grammar-green'>we</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>prepare</span> dinner is six o'clock.
</div>

**\*<span class='grammar-blue'>Verb.</span>**

Think about your kitchen or classroom. Try to describe one person, one tool, one dish, and one place with an adjective clause. Useful sentences help grammar stay in your memory.
