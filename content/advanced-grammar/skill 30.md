Title: Skill 30: Use Past Participles After Have and Had
Date: 2026-09-20
Category: Advanced Grammar
Tags: Advanced Grammar, TOEFL Preparation, Past Participles, Perfect Tenses
Summary: Choose the past participle after auxiliary have, has, or had in perfect tenses.

The **perfect tenses** use an **auxiliary verb** (**have**, **has**, or **had**) and a **past participle**. An auxiliary verb helps the main verb. A regular past participle usually ends in **-ed**. Irregular past participles, such as **eaten**, have other forms.

## Present perfect: have or has + past participle

Use **have** with *I, you, we,* and *they*; use **has** with *he, she,* and *it*. The **present perfect** connects a past action to the present.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
The cat <span class='grammar-red'>has</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>eaten</span> its food.<br>
I <span class='grammar-red'>have</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>seen</span> the movie before.<br>
The children <span class='grammar-red'>have</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>played</span> all morning.
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-red'>Auxiliary have or has.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-blue'>Past participle.</span>**
</sub>

**Eaten** and **seen** are irregular past participles; **played** is regular. In each sentence, the red auxiliary verb and the blue past participle form the present perfect verb phrase.

## Past perfect: had + past participle

Use **had** with any subject to place an action before another point in the past.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
The dog <span class='grammar-red'>had</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>eaten</span> before we <span class='grammar-blue'>came</span> home.<br>
Mia <span class='grammar-red'>had</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>finished</span> her book before dinner.
</div>

The dog ate before we came home. Mia finished her book before dinner. **Had eaten** and **had finished** show which actions happened first. **Had** is the auxiliary verb; **eaten** and **finished** are past participles.

## Do not confuse simple past with past participle

Some irregular verbs have different **simple past** and **past participle** forms: **eat → ate → eaten** and **see → saw → seen**. Use the past participle after the auxiliary **have**, **has**, or **had**.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
:x: The cat <span class='grammar-red'>has</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>ate</span> its food.<br>
:white_check_mark: The cat <span class='grammar-red'>has</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>eaten</span> its food.<br>
:white_check_mark: The cat <span class='grammar-blue'>ate</span> its food yesterday.<br>
:x: We <span class='grammar-red'>had</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>saw</span> the movie before it <span class='grammar-blue'>came</span> on TV.<br>
:white_check_mark: We <span class='grammar-red'>had</span> <span class='grammar-blue'>seen</span> the movie before it <span class='grammar-blue'>came</span> on TV.
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-red'>Auxiliary have, has, or had.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-blue'>Simple past verb or past participle.</span>**
</sub>

**Ate** and **saw** are simple past forms. **Eaten** and **seen** are past participles. The sentence **The cat ate its food yesterday** is correct because it uses the simple past without an auxiliary.

## Check the role of have

**Have** does not always form a perfect tense. In **I have a book**, **have** is the main verb. In **I have eaten**, **have** is an auxiliary verb and **eaten** is the past participle. Check the job of **have** before applying the rule.

## TOEFL strategy

Find **have**, **has**, or **had** and ask whether it is an auxiliary verb. If it is, check that the main verb is a past participle. Watch for irregular forms such as **eaten**, **seen**, and **gone**. Use **has** with a singular third person subject such as **Mia** or **the cat**.
