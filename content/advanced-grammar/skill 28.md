Title: Skill 28: Use Comparatives and Superlatives Correctly
Date: 2026-09-18
Category: Advanced Grammar
Tags: Advanced Grammar, TOEFL Preparation, Comparatives, Superlatives
Summary: Choose a comparative for two things and a superlative for one member of a group.

Once you know how to form **faster** and **the fastest**, you need to choose the right form. A **comparative adjective** compares two nouns. A **superlative adjective** describes one noun as the highest or lowest member of a group.

## Compare two things

Use a comparative adjective followed by **than** to compare two nouns. The nouns can name people, animals, places, or objects.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
<span class='grammar-orange'>A tiger</span> is <span class='grammar-red'>faster</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>than</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>a lion</span>.<br>
<span class='grammar-orange'>Tokyo</span> is <span class='grammar-red'>bigger</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>than</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>Paris</span>.
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-orange'>Noun phrases.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-red'>Comparative adjective.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-purple'>Comparison word.</span>**
</sub>

In the first sentence, the nouns **tiger** and **lion** are compared by speed. In the second, the cities **Tokyo** and **Paris** are compared by size. The comparative adjectives **faster** and **bigger** show the difference. **Than** introduces the second noun.

## Identify one member of a group

Use **the** plus a superlative adjective when one noun has the highest or lowest degree of a quality in a group. A phrase such as **in the class** names the group.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
<span class='grammar-orange'>The cheetah</span> is <span class='grammar-red'>the fastest</span> land animal <span class='grammar-pink'>in the world</span>.<br>
<span class='grammar-orange'>Mia</span> is <span class='grammar-red'>the youngest</span> student <span class='grammar-pink'>in the class</span>.
</div>
<sub>
**\*<span class='grammar-orange'>Noun phrase.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-red'>Superlative adjective with the definite article.</span>**<br>
**\*<span class='grammar-pink'>Comparison group.</span>**
</sub>

**Fastest** describes one land animal in a group of land animals. **Youngest** describes one student in a class. The pink phrases name the groups.

## Check that the form matches the meaning

Use a comparative adjective with **than** when the sentence compares two nouns. Use a superlative adjective when it names one noun as the highest or lowest in a group. Even if three vehicles are present, you can still compare just two of them.

<div class="grammar" markdown="1">
:x: <span class='grammar-orange'>The bus</span> is <span class='grammar-red'>the biggest</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>than</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>the car</span>.<br>
:white_check_mark: <span class='grammar-orange'>The bus</span> is <span class='grammar-red'>bigger</span> <span class='grammar-purple'>than</span> <span class='grammar-orange'>the car</span>.<br>
:x: <span class='grammar-orange'>The bus</span> is <span class='grammar-red'>bigger</span> <span class='grammar-pink'>of the three vehicles</span>.<br>
:white_check_mark: <span class='grammar-orange'>The bus</span> is <span class='grammar-red'>the biggest</span> <span class='grammar-pink'>of the three vehicles</span>.
</div>

In the first pair, **than the car** compares two vehicles. In the second pair, **of the three vehicles** names the group, so the adjective must be superlative.

## TOEFL strategy

Find the adjective first. If you see **than**, check for a comparative adjective. If the sentence identifies one noun in a group, check for **the** plus a superlative adjective. Make sure both sides of a comparison contain nouns of the same kind.
